The new jobs in IT, in plain words

DevOps, SRE, MLOps, FinOps: the titles multiply faster than anyone can explain them. Here is what each one actually does, and why it should matter to you even if you never write a line of code.

DisciplineWhat it isWhat it gives you

DevOps

Removes the wall between the people who write software and the people who run it. Code moves from a laptop to production through one automated, repeatable path.

New features reach customers in days rather than months, and releases stop being a risky event.

Platform engineering

Builds an internal “paved road” for developers: self-service environments, templates and tooling, so nobody waits on a ticket to get started.

A new project goes from idea to a running environment in an afternoon. Teams stop reinventing the wheel.

Site reliability engineering

Treats reliability as an engineering problem, with measurable targets, meaningful alerts and an on-call rotation that doesn’t exhaust people.

You know how reliable your service really is, incidents get shorter and rarer, and your team sleeps at night.

MLOps and LLMOps

Takes machine learning models and AI assistants out of notebooks and prototypes: versioning, automated deployment, monitoring for drift and cost.

Your AI investments actually reach production, and keep working as your data and your users change.

DevSecOps

Puts security checks inside the delivery pipeline instead of a review at the very end: dependency scanning, secrets management, policy as code.

Vulnerabilities are caught before release, and compliance evidence is produced automatically rather than assembled for each audit.

FinOps

Makes cloud spend visible, forecastable and owned by the teams that create it.

No more surprise invoices. Cost per product, per team or per customer becomes a number you can act on.

DataOps

Brings the automation, testing and monitoring that DevOps brought to application code to data pipelines.

Dashboards and reports you can trust, delivered on time, with problems caught before anyone decides on bad numbers.

How an engagement runs

Short, measurable steps. We work inside your teams rather than next to them, and we aim to leave you independent.

  1. Assess

    Two to three weeks with your teams: we map how software gets delivered today, where it hurts and what it costs. You get a written roadmap, whether or not you continue with us.

  2. Design

    We pick the smallest set of changes with the biggest effect, and agree on how to measure them: lead time, deployment frequency, time to recover, change failure rate.

  3. Build, alongside your team

    Our engineers work in your repositories and your rituals, pairing with your people rather than delivering slides.

  4. Hand over

    Documentation, training and a runbook for everything we touched. The goal is a team that no longer needs us.
